Optimising the First Fold

Most people never scrolled past the top fold of the screen.

So I optimised it to sell the services.

Role Design Point of Contact
Timeline Jul 3–20, 2026
Tools Figma, Microsoft Clarity
Status Shipped, live
Abstract

The problem.

Services were hidden below the fold.

The page opened with a big hero. Services were hidden below it, behind gray service tabs that made one option look already chosen. Most visitors never scrolled past this first screen.

The fix

Rebuilt the first fold to lead with services, equalized tab weight, and shipped a dev-ready page in under a month.

I. The Brief
Services are the product, not the story.

Meridian sells manuscript editing to researchers. Majority of traffic came from following channels:

Library

Majority of traffic comes from journal backlinks from Meridian's own online research library

Paid

Some part of traffic comes from paid campaigns and ads

Organic

Smaller share, direct Google search

I was the product designer and design point of contact. I owned this from the first research session to the shipped page, working closely with the PM, stakeholders, developers and marketing team. This case study covers that whole journey.

II. The Signal

Most visitors never scroll past the first fold

Microsoft Clarity insights showed a consistent pattern across visits: the large majority of users did not scroll past the first fold. Whatever content sat below roughly 900px was, for most sessions, effectively invisible.

→ If it isn't visible in the first screen, most visitors never see it.

The goal was clear: fix the first fold and reduce drop-offs.

III. The Diagnosis

The hero and the tabs, killed the fold.

The live page spent its one guaranteed screen on introductions instead of options. Two specific issues stood out:

1
2
Fig. 1 – Old version, first 900px The version live at the start of this project. Hero and intro copy run roughly 340px before any service is visible.
1

The hero swallowed the fold.

340px deep before a single service appeared on a page whose entire job was to sell services.

2

The tabs implied a decision before the visitor made one.

Three of four service tabs sat in flat gray on a gray field, reading as disabled.

III. The Fix

CUT THE HERO. SURFACE THE SERVICES.

By collapsing the marketing-focused hero and elevating our functional units directly to the top fold, users can discover and explore our concrete offerings immediately on arrival.

Fig. 2 – Shipped version, first 900px

Revision Details

Hero Before: 340px, full-bleed photo, no services visible After: 193px – a 43% cut, tab bar lands in view immediately below
Tab bar Before: Gray-on-gray inactive tabs, one implied default After: White background on every tab, one accent color marks the active state only
Service cards Before: Vertical stack – comparing all options meant real scrolling After: Two-column grid (stakeholder-agreed) – four services readable at a glance
Fold depth Before: ~1,300px to see all three services in the old layout After: 875px to see all four services, comfortably inside the first screen
Key Takeaways

THREE LESSONS FROM THIS OPTIMISATION.

I.

Attention is a scarce currency.

If visitors do not see what you do within the first 3 seconds, they will leave.

II.

Visual style should back function.

A beautiful design that hides your services is a failed marketing asset.

III.

Always design for real device heights.

Validate folds against standard viewport sizes, not just infinite design canvases.
